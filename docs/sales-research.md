# Sales Research — Coach Template

The source-of-truth document for who we're selling this template to, what pains they have, and what makes our template the answer. All claims are sourced; the agent that ran the research cited URLs we can revisit.

---

## The buyer (locked 2026-05-14)

A solo coach **1–4 years into their practice**. Has **10–40 paying clients**, mostly **1:1 packages** with maybe a small group program on the side. Monthly revenue around **$5k–$20k**. Currently runs their entire funnel through **Instagram, TikTok, or LinkedIn** — they post consistently, build an audience, and convert via DMs + Calendly.

Their website situation is either:

- **A bad website** — bought a $19 ThemeForest theme or a Squarespace template when they started, never replaced it. Loads slow, doesn't reflect who they've become, embarrassed to send their best leads there.
- **No website at all** — just a Calendly link in their bio. They know they need one but the prospect of building it is overwhelming.

**Why $497 isn't scary:** at $5k+ MRR, $497 is a fraction of one client's package. They're not asking "can I afford this" — they're asking "is this actually going to help me close more clients."

**Primary buyer for the headline = "bad-website" coach** (has urgency, losing leads now). **Secondary buyer addressed in FAQ = "no-website" coach** (lower urgency but no migration pain).

## Top 3 pains — what the sales copy leads with

These came up in 5+ independent sources, in real coach language. Sales-page headlines should pull from these.

### 1. The cringe-when-asked-for-the-URL pain

> *"that awkward hesitation when someone asks for your website link? The urge to say 'Oh, just ignore XYZ'... 'Oh, just outdated services page — I haven't had time to update it.' 'I know the design isn't great, but I promise I know what I'm talking about!'"*

Source: [Jess Creatives](https://jesscreatives.com/blog/that-cringey-feeling-when-someone-asks-for-your-website/)

The buyer's site is a fossil of who they were at zero clients — and they're now a year-3 coach with 30 clients sending leads to a year-1 website. The site **undermines the credibility they've already built.**

**Sales-copy lead candidate:** *"You hesitate when someone asks for your website link."*

### 2. The content treadmill that isn't converting

> *"You post and publish more. Maybe every day. Maybe multiple times per day. You just keep pumping out more and more content — even though it's not converting... What really wears us down is the disappointment that all that effort isn't translating to inquiries, clients, and income."*

Source: [Jana O. Media](https://janaomedia.com/content-burnout/)

**Content burnout is framed as the #1 reason coaches quit** — more than financial failure. Reframes our template's value: not just a prettier site, but a way *off* the treadmill (own your audience via email, get SEO traffic).

**Sales-copy lead candidate:** *"You're posting every day and your calendar still isn't filling up."*

### 3. Platform dependence anxiety

> *"You're subject to the platform's rules, algorithm changes, and the perpetual risk that shifts in policy could leave you disconnected from your followers overnight."*

Real example: *"This ban has directly affected my business and all of the hard work and branding that I've spent countless hours pouring into my business."*

Sources: [Memberful](https://memberful.com/blog/owned-vs-rented-audience), [SFist on Instagram mass suspensions](https://sfist.com/2025/06/16/instagram-users-complain-of-mass-suspensions-of-accounts-likely-by-ai/)

**Crucial nuance:** coaches don't *initially* feel this pain — they rationalize ("Instagram works for me") until something breaks. The copy has to **create the realization**, not assume they already have it. Citing real ban stories works as proof.

**Sales-copy lead candidate:** *"You're one Instagram algorithm change away from losing your funnel."*

## What our template fixes — pain ↔ section mapping

Every major pain has a section the template directly addresses. Sales-page bullet pattern: *pain → "Most coach sites do X. This template Y."*

| Pain | Template solution |
|---|---|
| Vague value prop ("I help people reach their full potential") | Hero section with desire→outcome headline pattern; Empathy section sharpens it further |
| Site is about the coach, not the client | Empathy section ("You want X. But right now you feel Y.") puts the visitor first |
| No clear next step on every page | "Free Discovery Call" CTA in sticky nav + multiple in-section soft CTAs |
| No email capture | Lead magnet section + Newsletter section, both wired to Kit |
| Locked in a builder invisible to Google | Next.js + SEO baseline (sitemap, structured data, OG) |
| Mobile speed bleeding visitors | Static rendering + image optimization = fast on mobile |
| Hidden pricing signals low confidence | Services section shows prices openly |
| Cringe-when-asked-for-URL | Premium typography + design that *exceeds* what a $20k/mo coach should have |

## Surprises worth using in the sales copy

These were unexpected and high-signal — worth specific callouts on the sales page.

### Shame language is much stronger than expected
Words to use: **"cringe," "apologize for," "hesitate."** NOT "your website could be better." The visceral pain is what coaches respond to.

### Content burnout > financial failure as #1 quitting reason
Sales-page hook: *"This template lets you stop being a content factory."* Coaches don't quit because they're broke. They quit because the content treadmill exhausts them faster than the business builds.

### The "I help people reach their full potential" cliché
Direct callout opportunity on the sales page:
> *"If your homepage says 'I help people reach their full potential,' your homepage is the problem."*
Specific. Memorable. Slightly aggressive in a way coaches will recognize.

### ICF (the credentialing body) endorses transparent pricing
Authority signal for defending the Services section. Cite: *"The ICF — the credentialing body for the entire coaching industry — lists hidden pricing as a top-5 mistake on coach websites."*

### Coaches rationalize platform dependence until it breaks
The sales copy has to *create* the realization, not assume it. Best lever: tell the story of someone whose account got banned. "Last June, hundreds of coaches woke up to find their Instagram accounts suspended overnight by a bad AI moderation sweep. They lost their entire client funnel in a single day." Source link to back it up.

## The pitch (locked 2026-05-14)

### Umbrella headline

> **"The website you'd pay an agency $5k to build, for $497."**

Anchors the price ($497 vs $5k = steal), implies all three differentiators, frames the comparison set as **agencies** (not other cheap templates).

### Three supporting differentiators (proof bullets)

These prove the headline. Used as the bulleted list directly under the hero on the sales page.

1. **You own the code.** No Wix subscription, no Squarespace lock-in, no platform that can lock you out. Migrate, modify, sell — it's yours.
2. **The funnel is engineered, not improvised.** 13 sections in the exact order Marie Forleo, Mel Robbins, and Amy Porterfield use. Built to convert, not just to look pretty.
3. **The infrastructure ships with it.** Lead magnet integration, sitemap, SEO metadata, mobile speed, structured data — built in, not bolted on.

### Competitor framing

Position **vs. agencies**, not vs. other templates. Reason: the agency frame is bolder, the math is more compelling, and it positions the buyer as someone "investing in their business" instead of "buying a cheap theme."

### Core message

**"This isn't a template. It's a finished website that just needs your name in it."**

Use this phrasing somewhere prominent on the sales page (maybe right under the headline, or in the "what makes this different" section).
